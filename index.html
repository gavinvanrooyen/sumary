00 / Mindset

Don't try to “win” the sceptic.

Use them to define what good looks like. In week one, I want the sceptical partner to tell me exactly what would make them change their mind — then turn that into measurable acceptance criteria.

My question: “What would Sumary have to prove in 90 days for you to say this is genuinely useful?”
“A small number of high-confidence alerts is more valuable than hundreds of possible anomalies.”
My design principle for the pilot
01 / 90-day plan

From pilot to proof.

Success at day 90 is not “15 users logged in”. It is evidence that Sumary catches real issues, reduces manual review, and fits naturally into the firm's workflow.

—signal quality
—active users
—rollout decision
Illustrative targets / demo values — not client results.

03 / What could kill the account?

I'd look for failure early.

By day 30 I want to know whether we have a product-value problem or an adoption problem.

01

False positives

Alerts outnumber useful findings. Seniors stop opening them.

Day-30 signal → dismissal rate
02

No workflow owner

Issues are surfaced but nobody knows who is expected to resolve them.

Day-30 signal → unresolved ageing
03

Champion dependency

The champion loves it, but the sceptic and neutral partner never adopt it.

Day-30 signal → stakeholder interviews
04 / Feed the product

Turn field friction into product signal.

After 90 days I would not send product a list of feature requests. I'd send patterns: what users ignore, what they still check manually, and where configuration keeps recurring.

Prioritisation: frequency × time saved × risk reduction × firms affected.

Would the sceptical partner renew?
